How Much Does a Mobile App Cost in Dubai? What Really Drives the Price

October 15, 2026 · 6 min read

Ask ten agencies what a mobile app costs and you will get ten very different answers. That is because the price depends on a handful of decisions you can control — features, platforms, design, integrations and what happens after launch. Rather than guess a number, this guide explains the factors that actually drive the price of a mobile app in Dubai so you can budget realistically and compare quotes fairly.

Why Quotes Vary So Much

A basic app with login, a catalogue and contact details is a completely different build from one with payments, live tracking, chat, role-based access and an admin dashboard. Quotes also vary because some include the backend, design and testing and others do not. Always ask what is in and what is out.

1. The Number of Features and Screens

Every feature adds design, development and testing time. Each screen has to be designed, built, connected to data and tested on many devices. The shortest way to control cost is a focused first version — see how to validate an app idea and the best business app features.

2. One Platform or Two

Building separate native apps for iOS and Android costs more than a single cross-platform codebase, and a progressive web app is lower again. Compare approaches in native vs cross-platform development and PWA vs native.

3. Design Complexity

Custom UI/UX, animations, illustration and Arabic right-to-left layouts take more effort than standard components. Good design is an investment in whether people keep using the app, so cut features before you cut usability.

4. Backend, Database and Admin Panel

Users never see the backend, but it holds your data, handles logins, sends notifications and powers the admin dashboard your team uses daily. Complex business logic, reporting and role permissions here can cost more than the screens themselves.

5. Integrations

  • Payment gateways and invoicing.
  • Maps, location and tracking.
  • WhatsApp, SMS and email notifications.
  • CRM, ERP, accounting and other business systems.

Each integration needs development, testing and sometimes third-party fees.

6. Launch and Running Costs

  • Apple and Google developer accounts.
  • Hosting, cloud services and third-party tools, often billed monthly.
  • Maintenance: bug fixes, security updates and compatibility with new operating system versions.
  • Improvements and new features based on user feedback.
  • Discoverability — see app store optimization.

How to Get a Fair Quote

  1. Write down your must-have features and your goal for the app.
  2. Ask each agency to break the quote into stages with deliverables.
  3. Clarify who owns the code, designs and store accounts.
  4. Ask what is not included: backend, admin panel, testing, hosting, maintenance.
  5. Compare scope first, price second.

A Worked Example of Scope Control

A restaurant group wants an ordering app with menu, cart, payments, delivery tracking, loyalty points, reviews and a kitchen dashboard. The cost and risk shrink dramatically if version one launches with menu, cart, payment and the kitchen dashboard, and loyalty, reviews and live tracking follow once ordering volume proves the demand. The business spends less before learning whether customers actually use the app.

Cost-Saving Moves That Do Not Hurt Quality

  • Reuse proven components for login, payments and notifications instead of building from scratch.
  • Launch one platform or a PWA first if your audience allows it, then expand.
  • Delay nice-to-have features such as loyalty, chat and referrals.
  • Prepare content early: product data, images, copy and Arabic translations arriving late create expensive rework.
  • Decide quickly: slow feedback extends the project and the budget.

Avoid savings that cost you later: skipping testing, ignoring security or leaving out the admin dashboard your team will need every day.

Questions to Ask Before You Sign

  • Does the price include design, development, backend, admin panel and testing?
  • Who owns the source code, designs and developer accounts?
  • What support is included after launch, and for how long?
  • How are changes to scope handled and priced?
  • Can you show similar apps you have built and connected to business systems?

Written answers to these questions will tell you more about a quote than the headline number.

Build Cost vs Total Cost of Ownership

The build is only part of what an app costs over its life. Over several years you will pay for hosting, third-party services, maintenance, updates for new phone operating systems, support and new features. A common planning approach is to reserve a yearly maintenance and improvement budget as a percentage of the original build, and to review it as the app grows. Ask your agency what a typical yearly cost looks like for an app like yours.

Think also about return: an app that increases repeat orders, cuts admin or brings new customers can justify higher spending. Set measurable goals before building so you can tell whether the investment worked.

Practical Tips From Real Projects

  • Write a one-page brief with your goal, users, must-have features and integrations before asking for quotes.
  • Prototype first. A clickable design is much cheaper to change than finished code.
  • Plan content early. Product data, images and translations delay many projects.
  • Agree a change process. Decide how new requests are estimated and approved.
  • Plan marketing. Budget for getting your first users, not just building the app.

Key Takeaways

  • App cost depends on features, platforms, design, backend, integrations and after-launch needs.
  • A focused first version lowers both cost and risk.
  • Compare quotes by scope, ownership and support, not just price.
  • Budget for maintenance and marketing, not only development.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do app quotes differ so much?

Different scope. Some quotes include only the visible app, while others include design, backend, admin panel, testing and launch support.

Is a cheap app a bad idea?

Not necessarily, but check what is included and who owns the code. Very cheap quotes often leave out backend work, testing or maintenance.

Can I start small and add features later?

Yes, and it is usually the smartest approach. Launch a focused first version and add features based on real usage.

Do I need to pay after the app launches?

Yes. Plan for hosting, updates, bug fixes and improvements. Apps need ongoing maintenance to keep working on new devices.
