Website Design Cost in Dubai: What Affects the Price in 2026

November 24, 2026 · 6 min read

Ask three agencies what a website costs in Dubai and you may get three very different quotes — from a few hundred dirhams to tens of thousands. The gap is rarely about greed. It is almost always about what is actually included: how many pages, how custom the design, which features, how much content and what happens after launch. This guide explains what really drives website design and development cost in the UAE so you can compare quotes properly and spend where it matters.

Why There Is No Single Price

A one-page landing page, a ten-page company website, an online store and a booking platform are different products. Each needs different design effort, features, testing and ongoing care. Anyone quoting a flat price without asking what you need is guessing — or leaving things out of the scope.

At Zenexio, professional business websites start from AED 699, and more complex builds are priced around the scope. The sections below explain why a price moves up or down, so you can see exactly what you are paying for.

1. Number of Pages and Amount of Content

More pages mean more design and development time, and more content to write, edit and approve. Also consider who creates the content: copywriting, photography, video and translation are often separate from the build cost. A site is only as good as its content, so plan for it early. Late content is also the most common reason projects overrun — see how long a website takes to build.

2. Design: Template or Custom

A template-based design is quicker and more affordable because the layout already exists and is adapted to your brand. A fully custom design built around your brand and audience takes longer but helps you stand out. Neither is wrong — it depends on how much your brand identity matters to your customers and how different you need to look from competitors.

3. Features and Functionality

  • Simple: contact forms, WhatsApp chat, Google Maps, image galleries.
  • Moderate: blogs, multi-language pages, enquiry forms with file upload, basic integrations.
  • Advanced: booking systems, customer portals, online payments, membership areas.
  • E-commerce: product management, checkout, shipping, tax and inventory logic.
  • Business systems: CRM integration, automation, custom dashboards and APIs.

Read our guide to the website features that generate leads to decide which ones are worth paying for first.

4. Arabic and Multilingual Support

Adding Arabic means more than translating text. The layout flips to right-to-left, fonts must be chosen carefully, and every form, menu and button has to be tested in both directions. Planning this from day one costs far less than retrofitting it later.

5. Platform and Technology

WordPress, a website builder, Shopify and custom code all price differently and suit different goals. Our comparison of website platforms for UAE businesses helps you pick the right one before you request quotes.

6. SEO, Speed and Security

A site that looks good but loads slowly or cannot be found on Google does not earn its cost. Performance work, search-friendly structure, SSL and basic security should be part of the build, not optional extras. See why speed is a design decision and basic website security steps.

Costs After Launch

  • Domain and hosting: usually renewed yearly.
  • Maintenance: updates, backups, security monitoring — see what maintenance should include.
  • Content and SEO: blog posts, new pages and optimisation to keep growing.
  • Marketing: ads and social media if you want traffic, not just a presence.

How to Compare Quotes Fairly

  1. Ask each agency for a written scope listing pages, features, revisions, languages, hosting and ownership of the code.
  2. Check what is excluded: content, photography, translation, plugins, licences.
  3. Ask how changes after approval are handled and priced.
  4. Check who owns the domain, hosting account and source files.
  5. Compare scope first, then price.

For a fuller checklist, read what a website development company should deliver.

A Worked Example

Two quotes arrive for a ten-page company website. Quote A is lower and lists "design and development". Quote B is higher and lists design, development, copy editing, Arabic version, forms connected to a CRM, speed optimisation, SEO setup, a launch checklist and three months of support. The cheaper quote is not cheaper if you then have to buy everything missing from it. Always line up the scope before comparing numbers.

Where to Invest and Where to Save

Invest in what customers notice and what earns revenue: clear messaging, strong mobile design, speed, trust signals, lead capture and integration with your sales process. Save on things that add little business value early on: complex animations, features nobody has asked for and pages created only to look bigger.

A good agency will challenge your wish list, explain trade-offs and help you build in phases. That is better value than the lowest number on a quote.

Practical Tips From Real Projects

  • Define your goal first. A website that exists to collect enquiries is designed differently from one that sells products.
  • Prioritise features. Separate must-have from nice-to-have and phase the rest.
  • Collect examples. Show the agency three sites you like and say why.
  • Ask about ownership. Confirm you will own your domain, content and code.
  • Plan for growth. Choose a structure that lets you add pages and features later without a rebuild.

Key Takeaways

  • Website cost is driven by pages, design, features, content, languages and platform.
  • Compare scope in writing before comparing price.
  • Budget for hosting, maintenance and content after launch.
  • Start with a focused version and grow in phases.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a website cost in Dubai?

It depends on the type of site, pages, design, features and content. Zenexio's website development starts from AED 699 for professional business sites, with more complex projects quoted around your requirements.

Why are website quotes so different?

Because scope differs. One quote may include content, SEO, Arabic and support while another includes only basic design.

Do I pay anything after the website is built?

Usually yes: domain and hosting renewals, and ideally maintenance and updates. Ask for these costs up front.

Is a cheap website a bad idea?

Not if it fits your goals and the scope is clear. Check ownership, speed, mobile design and what is included before you decide.
