How Long Does It Take to Build a Website in Dubai? Realistic Timelines for 2026

November 26, 2026 · 5 min read

One of the first questions every business asks is "when can we launch?" The honest answer is that it depends on the type of website, how quickly decisions are made and how ready your content is. A well-organised landing page can go live in days; a custom portal takes months. This guide gives realistic timelines for the most common projects, explains each stage of the process, shows what most often causes delays and offers practical ways to launch faster without cutting corners.

Typical Timelines by Project Type

These are general ranges for a well-organised project with prompt feedback. Your own timeline depends on scope, content readiness and how many people must approve each step.

Project typeTypical timelineMain time drivers
Landing pageAbout 1–2 weeksCopy, design approval, tracking setup
Small business website (5–10 pages)About 3–6 weeksContent, number of pages, Arabic version
Corporate website with custom designAbout 6–10 weeksCustom design rounds, CMS, integrations
E-commerce websiteAbout 8–16 weeksProduct data, payments, shipping, testing
Custom web application or portalSeveral months, in stagesRequirements, custom logic, security, testing

If an agency promises a complex site in a few days, ask exactly what is included. Speed is possible with templates; quality custom work needs time.

The Stages That Take Time

  1. Discovery and planning: goals, audience, pages, features and sitemap.
  2. Design: layouts and visual style for desktop and mobile, with rounds of feedback.
  3. Development: turning approved designs into a working site and connecting forms and integrations.
  4. Content: writing, photos, translation and uploading, which often runs in parallel.
  5. Testing: devices, browsers, forms, speed and security.
  6. Launch: going live, redirects, analytics and search engine setup.

For the final stage, use our website launch checklist so nothing is missed.

What Most Often Causes Delays

  • Late content. Copy, logos and photos arriving after design is finished is the number one cause.
  • Slow feedback. A week to respond to each design round adds weeks to the project.
  • Changing scope. Adding features mid-build means redesign, rebuilding and retesting.
  • Too many decision-makers. Conflicting opinions stall approvals.
  • Third-party dependencies. Payment gateway approvals, domain access, or integrations with other systems.

How to Launch Faster Without Cutting Corners

  • Prepare your content before the project starts.
  • Appoint one person who can approve work for the whole business.
  • Launch a focused first version, then add features after go-live.
  • Agree the scope in writing, including how changes are handled.
  • Book feedback sessions in advance so approvals do not wait on calendars.

This phased approach also keeps early costs down. See what affects website design cost in Dubai for how scope drives price as well as time.

Arabic, E-Commerce and Integrations Add Time

Right-to-left Arabic layouts, product catalogues, payment gateways and CRM integrations each add design, development and testing work. They are worth planning for from the start rather than retrofitting. Our comparison of website platforms shows how the platform choice affects build time.

A Worked Example

Two companies each need a ten-page website. Company A sends all content in week one, nominates one approver and replies to design drafts within a day. It launches in five weeks. Company B sends content page by page, involves four decision-makers and replies after a week each time. The same project takes twelve weeks. The difference had nothing to do with the agency — it was preparation and decision speed.

Fixed Launch Dates: How to Work Backwards

If you must launch by a certain date — a campaign, an exhibition or a seasonal peak — work backwards. Subtract time for launch and testing, development, content and design approval, and you will see the latest date the project can start. If the answer is "last week", reduce scope: launch fewer pages, postpone advanced features and add them after go-live.

A focused first release is nearly always better than a rushed complete one. Visitors cannot tell what you left out, but they can tell when something is broken.

Practical Tips From Real Projects

  • Gather content before kickoff. Collect logos, photos, team details and draft text so design is based on real content, not placeholders.
  • Pick one decision-maker. Collect feedback from the team, but let one person give final approval.
  • Review in batches. Give consolidated feedback once per round instead of piecemeal comments.
  • Plan Arabic early. Decide at the start which pages need translation and who will review it.
  • Leave a buffer. Allow extra days for testing and unexpected issues before any fixed launch date, such as an event or campaign.

Key Takeaways

  • Timelines depend on site type: days for landing pages, weeks for business sites, months for custom platforms.
  • Late content and slow feedback cause most delays.
  • A phased launch gets you live sooner and keeps risk low.
  • Work backwards from fixed dates and cut scope instead of quality.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a website take to build?

A landing page can take one to two weeks, a small business site three to six, a corporate site six to ten, and e-commerce or custom platforms longer. Content and feedback speed are the biggest factors.

Can a website be built in a week?

A simple landing page can, with ready content and quick approvals. A multi-page business site usually needs more time to do properly.

What slows website projects down most?

Late content, slow feedback and changes in scope after design is approved.

Does Arabic add time?

Yes. Right-to-left layouts, translation review and testing in both directions add design and testing effort.
